I feel that there's something about the pieces of this system that tell more about the whole.
And there's something about polarity and polarization that I think has not been tapped into
yet, is not fully understood, and will shed an inordinate amount of light on how the system has
to be communicating. You can actually run experiments on living neurons through software
nowadays, which is interesting, and I'm wondering what would be the correct way to go about
this.

I think that for a system this complex, that still seems to work so well in so many cases, if
we can understand this, then we can understand the obvious ways that we're causing
dysregulation of the systems. For example, we as a society have so quickly adopted all of these
different wavelengths and electromagnetic devices. We're concerned about the chemical pollution
of the planet. But we're not concerned about electromagnetic pollution and what it implies.

I'm just wondering about cells, which in one form or another make up all living things. We can
see that they use bioelectricity as one of the many ways they run. I'm not talking about
magnetoreception or electroreception. I'm strictly talking about a cell reading its own
bioelectric field and its neighboring cells, which are descendants of the same cell, and being
able to know the status and the position of another part of the body, and then infer things
from that. Maybe memory. Maybe history.

For example, melatonin is regulated by light. So there's an external clock, the sun, that talks
to our internal circadian clock, and there are changes associated with it. So what if our
internal clock uses that reference to locate memory? Like, to work out where the memories are
sitting in the body, and how the neurons need to rearrange to achieve retrieval of a memory.
And because we have so many things interfering with that light, you have a weaker system, a
system that's not as good at geometrically rearranging itself.

There's cymatics, where vibration arranges matter into geometric patterns, and the idea that
nature rearranges itself geometrically when several influences combine. What if the body uses
these multiple modalities not as a convenience but as a multi-step fallback relay system? A
system that is required for understanding not just the world around us, but also the memories
and the gene expression. A system that is built dependent on, and connected to, and very aware
of the planet, the environment that we live in, as far as where the sun is in the sky
indicating how the body should be working and what state it should be in. So that the neurons
know where to look for things, and how to send a signal and receive it back as an echo or a
relay, and know the state of that other system compared to their own, because they can compare
the two different modalities that they sent the signal by, or three different modalities.

And the extracellular activity that we're seeing is actually not just cells firing in unison,
but rearranging as well. Maybe if we could see at that level: when the heart emits a frequency
that the neurons in the nucleus of the solitary tract (NTS) or in the insula recognize, they
know it as one of the parts of the salience network. It knows that part. It knows how long it
took for that signal to get there, and it also knows that it took a little bit too long, or
it's dampened, or the shape of the signal has changed. And so then it rearranges the neurons
and fires in such a way that sends another message.

Cells also have mechanical and electrical resonances. And so these are all relay systems. They
fire and can be fired by those same frequencies, and there's a cascading that happens. It's not
all random. There has to be something that glues this together.
